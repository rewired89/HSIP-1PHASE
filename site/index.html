A notary for AI decisions

HSIP gives companies tamper-evident, independently verifiable proof of what their automated systems decided and when.

Check a proof in your browser Ask about a pilot Source code

The problem

Companies let automated systems and AI agents act on their behalf. When something goes wrong, there is rarely a neutral record of what the system did, when, or under whose authority. A log kept by the company that owns it can be edited, so it carries little weight in an audit or a dispute.

How it works

  1. Record. Your system sends a SHA-256 fingerprint of each decision. The content of your decisions never reaches HSIP.
  2. Sign and chain. HSIP signs the record and links it to the one before it.
  3. Anchor. Records are batched about every minute into a Merkle tree. The signed root can also be timestamped on Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps.
  4. Verify. Anyone, including an auditor or a court expert, can check the record offline with open source code, without trusting us.

Permissions for agents

You can sign a permission that says what an agent is allowed to do, then check recorded actions against it. You make and keep the key yourself, and the Proof Check page can create one in your browser.

Try it now

No account is needed. Open the Proof Check page, or download a sample proof and a sample evidence pack and check them yourself. The page makes no network requests after it loads.

What it does not do

  • A proof shows that a record existed, unaltered, at a given time. It does not show the decision was right, and it does not make a system compliant with any rule.
  • HSIP records and proves. It does not yet stop an agent before it acts. That is the next thing to build.
  • By default the platform holds each organization's signing key. You can switch to a key you hold yourself, and that switch is one-way.
  • No independent third-party security audit has been completed yet.
  • The platform runs from the open source today. Pilots are set up by arrangement, and there is no self-serve signup yet.

The full list is in the platform guide, and the wire format is in the proof format specification.
